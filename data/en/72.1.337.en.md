Volost Administration of the Three-Ostrov [Volost] of the Balashov District. 30 January 1858, No. 119.

To the Chief of the Balashov District.

> [in margin] On the disorders that occurred during the balloting of the members of the Volost Administration for the new three-year term.

The Volost Head of this Administration, in his proposal of the 20th of this January, No. 1, explained that at the beginning of the balloting of candidates for the office of Assessor for the police department for the new three-year term from 1858 to 1861 and of the trustworthy men of the Volost Court, at the present Volost assembly the peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka Anofry Sivolapov stepped forward from among the peasants and announced that they do not wish to ballot the assessors because the candidates for the office of Volost Head have [not] been balloted, whereas meanwhile the candidates for assessor for the Peschanka Society had already been balloted. Toward the end of the whole balloting, the peasant Semyon Trofimov Vyskubov came to the assembly and, together with the peasants Anofry Sivolapov, Andrey Zakharenkov, Fyodor Veslenikov, Yefrem Bondarenkov, Danila Kravtsov, Yefrem Korsunov, Nikita Shcherbakov, and Yakov Sorokin — who had appeared privately and was not among the elected peasants — insistently demanded of Head Bozhkov that he ballot the said peasant Semyon Vyskubov for the office of Volost Head, by which they disturbed the quiet of the whole assembly; and when Head Bozhkov refused them in this demand, then the above-mentioned peasants intended to ballot Vyskubov by forcible means. In consequence of which, upon the conclusion of the balloting, Head Bozhkov ordered the balloting box with balls to be taken from them, and then the disturbers of the peace entered the Volost Administration [and] demanded an inkwell and paper to make a list of the old men wishing to ballot the peasant Vyskubov, which was not refused them; and therefore he proposes to bring this to the knowledge of Your High Nobility.

Of which the Volost Administration, in addition to its report of the 20th of this January, No. 64, has the honor herewith, for your dependent disposition, to report to Your High Nobility. The original was written by Volost Head Bozhkov, countersigned by Volost Clerk Maslenikov.

District Chief A. ...

No. 2411.

Administration of the Saratov Chamber of State Domains. 21 January 1858, No. 116.

To the Saratov Civil Governor.

> [in margin] On the disorders in the Samoylovka Society.

The Balashov District Chief, on the 24th of last December, No. 4252, reported to me that in the month of November, in accordance with the disposition of the Chamber, elections were held for the offices of Volost and Village officials for the coming three-year term in the Three-Ostrov Volost, at which the election of candidates for the village offices was carried out with observance of the established order; then on the same day, 20 November, the preliminary candidates for the volost offices were also [elected], but the balloting of these candidates was deferred, on account of the late hour, to the following day, on which, during the balloting of the three candidates designated the day before for the office of Volost Head, two of them were rejected. Wherefore the Village Elder proposed to the village assembly to elect candidates anew for the said office. On this matter a consultation took place among the electors, during which there came to the assembly, straight from the road, returning from Saratov, the peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka Semyon Vyskubov and, addressing the electors, announced to them that the case for which he had been handed over to the criminal court was concluded and that he was completely acquitted, in attestation [of which] he produced what he said was a decree of the Saratov Criminal Chamber given to him. Vyskubov's appearance at the assembly and his described action had as their consequence that some of the electors, disposed in Vyskubov's favor by various relations, expressed the wish to designate Vyskubov as a candidate for the office of Volost Head. On this account the Village Elder, having required from Vyskubov the decree of the Criminal Chamber he had shown to the assembly, examined it and found that the paper produced by Vyskubov was a note given to him from the Chancellery of the Criminal Chamber [stating] that he had appeared in it for examination upon the Chamber's summons, and [that] there was now no need of him. Of which the Elder, informing the assembly, announced that Vyskubov, as one under trial, could not be balloted for any office, whereupon he read to the electors the decree of the Chamber of State Domains of [illegible] August of last year, No. 5, from which it is evident that Vyskubov had been handed over to trial for evasion of entering the office of tax collector and for disobedience to the Authorities, together with [illegible].

The Elder then ordered Vyskubov to withdraw from the assembly, as having no right to be present at it. After this it was again proposed to the assembly to designate and elect candidates, but the greater part of the electors, declaring that they could designate no one as candidate except Vyskubov, demanded that he be admitted to the balloting. Although the Elder tried to persuade the electors remaining on Vyskubov's side of the unlawfulness of their demand, all his persuasions not only did not incline the electors to carry out his admonition, but disposed them still more in Vyskubov's favor. In consequence of which, and to prevent any disorder upon the further removal of Vyskubov from the balloting, the Elder and the electors who shared his opinion, yielding to the insistence of the peasants devoted to Vyskubov, whose number was greater — out of 312 [men], up to [illegible] men — unwillingly admitted him to the balloting, by which he was chosen senior candidate for the office of Volost Head. After this the last candidate was designated and balloted, and then the village elections were concluded, of the consequences of which the Village Elder immediately informed the Volost Administration, which, bearing in mind that of the candidates elected for the office of Volost Head by the Samoylovka Society two — Semyon Vyskubov and David Bondarenkov — were: the first under trial on the above-stated matter, and the second under investigation for the appropriation of a horse belonging to the Balashov townsman Moisey Kozhevnikov [?], and being guided by Articles 46 and 56 of the regulations on elections, disposed by proposal to the village assembly to conduct an additional balloting for the election of the proper number of candidates for the office of Volost Head. This was announced by the Elder to the electors, but those of them devoted to Vyskubov refused to carry out the proposal of the Volost Administration under the pretext that the balloting they had conducted was lawful. Having received word of this, the District Chief personally admonished the electors as to the correctness of the Volost Administration's disposition, [but] for all his persuasions the electors, [against] the removal of Vyskubov, do not agree to the election of the indicated candidates, putting forward as their reason for this [illegible] and adding that they are ready to conduct the Volost balloting, provided, however, that Vyskubov be admitted to the balloting.

Wishing to make clear to the said electors their mistaken conviction in the possibility of appointing Vyskubov to the office of Volost Head — in consequence of which, as he, Mr. Volotsky, ascertained, they covertly evade designating other candidates — Mr. Volotsky repeatedly, personally and through his Assistant, repeated his admonitions to them, even with the aid of the parish Priest, but all these persuasions had no success; and, supposing the cause of this to be Semyon Vyskubov's seeking of the office of Volost Head, through which he, with the aid of his own brother Ilya and other associates close to him, having gained the favor of many electors, holds them to his benefit, Mr. Volotsky directed [his measures] against Vyskubov, to the suppression of the disorder aroused by their machinations, but by this measure too achieved no success, because Vyskubov and many electors have the unwavering wish that Semyon Vyskubov serve as Volost Head; however, by his actions Mr. Volotsky brought the peasants to acknowledge that they would change their wish concerning Vyskubov if it should not please me to appoint him Volost Head, and that no other admonition can serve to convince them of the impossibility of appointing Vyskubov to the office of Volost Head, since they find him worthy of it and wish by this appointment to vindicate him before the authorities for the act for which he was handed over to trial.

For the restoration of order in the settlement of Samoylovka, Mr. Rezanovich, Assessor of the Chamber, was dispatched, with [instructions] that, upon his arrival on the spot, he should admonish the peasants of their unlawful actions and conduct the elections on a lawful basis, reporting on the consequences of his action. In fulfillment of this, Mr. Rezanovich, by his report of the 14th of this January, reported that upon his arrival in the settlement of Samoylovka he summoned to himself the peasants Vyskubov and Bondarenkov, who had been balloted at the village assembly as candidates for the office of Volost Head, and by persuasion succeeded in inclining them to renounce the balloting, to which they have no right, and to ask the electors of the society to leave them in peace and not by their obstinacy bring themselves into liability. At the full assembly, which was gathered on the 1st, he explained to the electors the penalty to which they would be subjected if they should still be obstinate in their unlawful demand, and to Vyskubov and Bondarenkov — the punishment for their stirring up of the society; then he ordered both of them, Vyskubov and Bondarenkov, to ask the society to conduct a new balloting, but to all their requests the electors, numbering from 1312 down to 260, shouted that they had already balloted them, and would not do another balloting. After this some of the electors began to ask Mr. Rezanovich to permit them to give Vyskubov and Bondarenkov a recommendation as to conduct, so that they might present those recommendations to the court, as required, and for the vindication of the first from under trial and the second from under investigation; and this request, after the first, the rest also began to express. Seeing the electors' common wish with them and not finding this objectionable — the more so as, during the conduct of the investigation of both, the electors had recommended them when giving testimony — supposing that with this the electors' obstinacy regarding the balloting would cease, he permitted the recommendations to be written, which were given to Vyskubov and Bondarenkov; and, in order all the more to draw the electors to the fulfillment of their duty, he ordered a prayer service to be held, after which he told them that they had done all they could for their fellow-villagers Vyskubov and Bondarenkov, having given them recommendations, and [that] they should now proceed to the balloting of new candidates. To this there were no objections, wherefore it was ordered that the box be brought out to begin the balloting; but no sooner had it been brought out than three electors — Yefrem Bondarenkov, a fellow-villager of the one being balloted, Spiridon Shakhmatov, and Yefim Korsunov — began to say that they did not wish to proceed to the balloting; their words stirred up the others as well, except for a few who, not cherishing any special attachment to Vyskubov and Bondarenkov, wished to conduct a new balloting. Thereafter all his, Mr. Rezanovich's, persuasions remained unsuccessful, and, not having conducted the elections, having dismissed the assembly, he ordered that on the next day the electors of the volost assembly be gathered for the balloting of 3 trustworthy men [and] Assessors of the Volost Court, for whom the candidates had already been chosen. At this assembly the electors proceeded to the balloting of the candidates for Assessor, and, having chosen one, he left the settlement of Samoylovka.

Following this report, the Balashov District Chief, on 17 January, reports that although he continued his actions toward the suppression of the disorder that had occurred in the settlement of Samoylovka, this measure remained unsuccessful: the peasants remain firm in their obstinacy, and it is in no way weakened either by the measure he undertook or by the actions of the Chamber's Assessor. From the information gathered it appears that the peasants have allowed themselves such disobedience not for the first time: at several balloting already they had violated, in greater or lesser degree, the order established for elections. Of these instances, the conduct of the peasants at the balloting of 1848 draws particular attention, in which they showed stubborn disobedience to the Authorities. This conduct is confirmed by official proceedings and is explained in detail in the report to the Chamber by the former District Chief of 29 January 1849, No. 229. In that instance the peasants' disobedience reached obstinacy in the highest degree and was suppressed, after prolonged and unsuccessful admonition by the local administration's authorities, only by the personal action on the spot of the Chamber's Administrator, Mr. Bodisko.

> [in margin] From the archival case.

From the archival case, drawn up upon the report of the Balashov District Chief of 29 January 1849, No. 229, it is evident that the peasants of the settlements of Samoylovka and Peschanka, at the balloting for the three-year term beginning in 1849, elected only candidates for the offices of village administration, but did not elect candidates for the Volost offices, and instead appeared directly at the Volost assembly, in number 213 men, [and] by voice elected [men] to the offices of the Volost Administration. The Volost Administration, considering the election unlawful, proposed conducting a new balloting, but the villagers refused this; and when this was reported to the District Chief, then the District Chief at that time, Nesselshef [?], went to the settlements of Peschanka and Samoylovka, where, having gathered the electors of the societies and the people of the assemblies, he read to them the rules existing on this matter and admonished them that the elections they had made to the offices of Volost officials were not in accordance with the rules indicated in the regulations, insisting that these elections be made anew. For all his persuasions, the electors, except for 12 men, refusing to enter upon new elections, declared to him that at the first [elections], had it been proposed to them how to do them, they would have carried out everything prescribed, [but] because, considering the election to office just, they did not express agreement to conducting new ones, adding moreover that this disorder had arisen from the Volost Clerk Veliky, who, having appeared at the assembly drunk, could not explain to those balloting how they were to proceed, and from the peasant Ilya Vyskubov, who had been designated as senior candidate for Volost Head, [who] the latter, as Mr. Shener [?] ascertained covertly, had supposedly promised not to collect the taxes in a timely manner and to issue them the grain they requested from the granaries immediately upon entering office; for this reason the peasants probably did not wish to conduct new elections, wishing to keep Vyskubov as Volost Head.

Presenting all the above to Your Excellency, I have the honor most humbly to request that you make a disposition both for the conduct of a formal investigation and for the placing under guard of the chief instigators of the disorders, who showed manifest disobedience to the Authorities and disturbed the quiet and peace at the assembly, for such a measure, the more so in my opinion, may serve to remove the agitation of the peasants and to bring them into proper obedience to the authorities and to quiet; and as such disorder in the balloting of candidates occurs in the Three-Ostrov Volost not for the first time, I have the honor most humbly to request the consent of Your Excellency to a representation about this to the Minister of State Domains and to a request for the permission of His Excellency that, if even admonitions do not incline the societies to the election of candidates for Head, then a Volost Head be appointed, without their wish, from among retired soldiers or peasants of that society, and that for disobedience to the authorities and manifest obstinacy in this case they be deprived of the right of elections for at least two three-year terms.

[Signed] Acting [illegible] of the Chamber [illegible]. [Countersigned] Office Manager A. [illegible].

On the 3rd day of March 1858, the Official for Special Commissions of the Chief of Saratov Province Sivtsov and the Balashov District Chief Volotsky, in consequence of the prescription of His Excellency of the 19th of last February, No. 573, having arrived on this date in the settlement of Samoylovka of the Balashov District for the conduct of the investigation into the disorders made by the state peasants of that settlement at the elections to the offices of Volost and Village officials for the coming three-year term, examined the case begun on this matter and found it necessary: to require from the Three-Ostrov Volost Administration a list of the elected peasants of the Village 1st [assembly] of the Samoylovka Society and of the Volost assemblies, with an indication of which of these electors expressed willingness to conduct, at the demand of the Authorities, an additional balloting in the Samoylovka Society for the election of candidates for the office of Volost Head; and 2nd, for the closer resolution of the circumstances of the case with regard to the cause that prompted the villagers of the settlement of Samoylovka to the persistent designation of the peasant Semyon Vyskubov for the office of Volost Head, and to bring into greater knowledge all those persons who, before others, acted in Vyskubov's favor — to gather from the Volost Head Bozhkov and the Samoylovka Village Elder the following information: a) whether, after the actions of Mr. Assessor of the Chamber of State Domains and of the District Chief, new circumstances were discovered by them in this case that might serve to clarify the direct cause that turned the villagers in favor of Semyon Vyskubov, and b) concerning those persons who are not yet known in the case but who assisted in the election of Vyskubov, taking part in it either secretly or openly at the assembly, not having the right to be present among the electors; and therefore they resolved: having recorded the above in the journal, to carry it into execution, and then, upon receipt from the Volost Administration of the said lists and from the elder and electors of the explained information, to proceed to the further conduct of the investigation, through the questioning of the chief electors of the village assembly who expressed willingness to conduct the additional balloting, with the aim that, the testimonies given by them being entitled to greater credence, the data be positively determined which may serve to clarify the direct cause that turned a part of the electors in favor of Semyon Vyskubov.

> [in margin] Executed [illegible].

Official for Special Commissions Sivtsov. District Chief [Volotsky].

To the Three-Ostrov Volost Head Bozhkov. Inquiry.

For the investigation now being conducted into the disorders made by the state peasants of the Three-Ostrov Volost at the elections to the offices of Volost and Village officials for the coming three-year term, you are to furnish without delay, upon this inquiry, the following information: 1) whether new circumstances were discovered in this case, after the actions of Mr. Assessor of the Chamber of State Domains and of the District Chief, that might serve to clarify the direct cause that turned the villagers in favor of Semyon Vyskubov; 2) concerning those persons who are not yet known in the case but who assisted in the election of Vyskubov, taking part in it secretly or openly at the assembly, not having the right to be present among the electors. 3 March 1858.

Official for Special Commissions Sivtsov.

---

In reply to the inquiry of Your High Nobility in the case of the disorders made by the state peasants of the Three-Ostrov Volost at the elections to the offices of Volost and Village officials for the present three-year term, I have the honor to explain: no new positive circumstances have been discovered, except those of which the authorities have already been officially informed and which were brought to light by the personal action of the Assessor of the Saratov Chamber of State Domains and the district chief. But from the noted actions of the peasants Ilya and Semyon Vyskubov, of whom the first appeared during the elections at the village assembly without need, having no right to do so, being a non-elector and under trial, and besides was noticed in conversations with the electors, and the latter likewise allowed himself, without any compelling reason, to appear at the village and volost assemblies and to announce his release from under trial, — it must be supposed that they, the Vyskubovs, were seeking to occupy the office of Volost Head, the more so as the first was noticed in this at the balloting of 1849, and the latter did not keep the promise he gave to the Chamber's Assessor to renounce this office. In this seeking the Vyskubovs were aided, as they showed themselves by their actions, by the peasants of the settlement of Samoylovka: Yefrem Bondarenkov, Yefrem Korsunov, Danila Kravtsov, Yakov Sharkov, Zakhar Natalichev, Danila Sayenkov, Dmitry Ryasnyansky, Anofry Sivolapov, Fyodor Bebeshkov, Spiridon Shakhmatov, and Yakov Sorokin, who appeared privately at the assembly and was not among the electors. The Vyskubovs succeeded in gaining the favor of many electors, as must be supposed, not without some indulgent promise in favor of the villagers, to which suspicion is also led by the same action of Ilya Vyskubov at the balloting of 1849; whereto I add that of the peasants named above Spiridon Shakhmatov, after the admonition of the authorities, withdrew from the Vyskubovs' party. March the 3rd day 1858.

Volost Head Bozhkov.

1. He is called Yelisey, surnamed Pestichev, 75 years old, of the Orthodox faith, attends confession and Holy Communion, illiterate, has not been under trial or investigation and has not been punished for anything, peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka, [I] am an elector of the society for the village assemblies. During the balloting in 1857 of the candidates for the office of Volost Head of our volost, when a consultation was taking place among us electors as to which exactly of the worthy men ought to be elected to this office, there appeared at the assembly, without being summoned and not being an elector, the peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka Semyon Vyskubov, and announced to the assembly that the case for which he is under trial was concluded and that he had been acquitted; in attestation of this he showed some paper, calling it a decree of the Criminal Chamber. In consequence of which the greater part of the electors, disposed, I do not know why, in Vyskubov's favor, expressed the wish to ballot him, Vyskubov, as a candidate for Volost Head, and, despite all the admonitions of the Elder and the Volost Head that Vyskubov, being under trial, could not by law be Volost Head, those electors, and especially Yefrem Bondarenkov, Spiridon Shakhmatov, and Yefrem Korsunov among them, having disturbed the order and peace at the assembly, insistently demanded to ballot Semyon Vyskubov for Volost Head and would not ballot anyone except him and David Bondarenkov. Therefore, insistently, not heeding many electors, they arbitrarily began and conducted the balloting, in which, although I also took part, it was only so as not to arouse still greater disorder. Then the rejected electors were repeatedly given fresh admonition about their incorrect action and that they should conduct an additional balloting, but they, with the exception of myself and a few others, did not express agreement to it and did not carry out the demand either of the local officials, or of the District Chief, or even of the Assessor of the Chamber of State Domains. What exactly the cause was that prompted the 37 electors to the described action, I cannot explain, for I did not take part in their consultations and heard nothing about it; in disobedience to the authorities, as also in objecting to a new balloting of candidates for Volost Head, I did nothing, but on the contrary I always agreed to all the proposals of the authorities and now do not refuse to ballot both candidates for Volost Head and the other village and volost offices, whomever the society and the authorities may propose. He is called Semyon, son of Trofim, surnamed Vyskubov, 32 [?] years old, married, of the Orthodox faith, attends confession and Holy Communion, literate, is under trial for evasion of entering the office of tax collector and disobedience to the Authorities, peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka, where I am also recorded according to the registers of the 9th census. At the village balloting on 20 November of last year 1857 I came not to the assembly, but was in the shop of the Merchant Skorikov, situated near the place where the village Court assembly was, and from there I went into the court office, because I had returned home from Saratov. The Village Clerk Nikolay Petrov Sokolov, knowing that I am under trial, demanded proof, and on 21 November I produced the said paper from the Criminal Chamber, which I showed him; he, having read it, said nothing and gave it back to me, wherefore I went home, and what happened afterward at the assembly I know nothing of. Then there came to our settlement of Samoylovka the District Chief Mr. Volotsky and the Assessor of the Chamber of State Domains, who told me to announce to the electors that, being under trial, I could not be Head; this was done by me. At that time, in my presence, the electors made no disorder or noise, but afterward I heard that someone among them, even before my arrival, had demanded of the Head to ballot first the Volost Head and then the Assessors and Trustworthy men, but whether this demand of theirs was heeded and what took place there among them, I know nothing of. As for my being balloted for Volost Head, I asked no one, and likewise my brother Ilya asked no one about it; I do not wish to serve as Head, and I asked the society and the electors for my dismissal from this office in the presence of the Assessor of the Chamber of State Domains. To this examination Semyon Trofimov [Vyskubov], peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka, set his hand. Official for Special Commissions Sivtsov. District Chief [Volotsky].

On the 7th day of March 1858, the Official for Special Commissions of the Chief of Saratov Province Sivtsov and the Balashov District Chief Volotsky, in consequence of the reports of the settlement of Samoylovka's police constable Sivolapov and of the Village Elder Troshchilov of the 7th of this March, examined the investigation being conducted into the disorders in the Three-Ostrov Volost at the election of officials and, having regard to the circumstances of this case, resolved: 1) As it is evident from the reports of [constable] Sivolapov and Elder Troshchilov that all the persons subject to further questioning in the present case, up to 50 men, are not at their residence, and the time of their return, except for the Clerk Sokolov, is unknown, therefore to suspend the conduct of the investigation until the summons and appearance of the said persons, of which proper requisitions are to be made to whom they belong, and upon receipt of notifications of the arrival of the absent persons to set out again without delay for the settlement of Samoylovka to complete this investigation. 2) From the investigation conducted, discrepancy arises among the questioned persons in the chief circumstances relating to the disorder at the balloting, namely: between the officials and the villagers of the smaller party who confirm their statements — against the peasants of the larger party, and finally among the latter themselves. To clarify these discrepancies it is necessary to give the said persons a confrontation, but as the testimonies of the persons remaining unquestioned in the case must relate to these same subjects, therefore in the explained discrepancies a confrontation should be given upon the final examination of all the persons connected with the case, with the aim that, through a premature comparison of the discrepancies that have occurred, the guilty not be given the opportunity to conceal the truth in the case through collusion. 3) From the circumstances of the investigation conducted it is evident that more guilty than others in the disorder that occurred at the balloting appear the peasants of the settlement of Samoylovka: Mikhayla Kravtsov, Yefrem Bondarenkov, Yakov Sharkov, Dmitry Ryasnyansky, Zakhar Natalichev, Yefrem Korsunov, Anofry Sivolapov, Danila Kravtsov, Fyodor Bebeshkov, Yakov Sorokin, Vasily Khoroshevsky, Danila Sayenkov, and Semyon, Ilya, and Ivan Vyskubov. These peasants were the acting persons in the said disorder, and therefore it is necessary, for the restoration of peace in the society and to prevent further action of these peasants toward concealing the truth in the case, to remove them for the time of the conduct of the investigation from the society; but in order to have beforehand a closer clarification of the degree of guilt of these peasants and of their manner of life, to question now, as the case requires, the elector peasants of the settlement of Peschanka as witnesses and to make a general inquest concerning the conduct of the said peasants, together with an investigation of their action, and then to examine the case anew according to its circumstances and upon it to draw up a resolution on the manner of arrest of the connected peasants and in general on the measures for the suppression of their action and for the restoration of peace in the society. 4) To record all the above in the Journal and to carry it into execution. On the 10th day of March 1858, the Official for Special Commissions of the Chief of Saratov Province Sivtsov and the Balashov District Chief Volotsky, examining the investigation being conducted into the disorders in the Three-Ostrov Volost at the election and balloting of officials, found that from the circumstances of this case the following is evident:

The Village and Volost authorities of the Three-Ostrov Volost, and then the District Chief Volotsky and the Assessor of the Saratov Chamber of State Domains Mr. Rezanovich, in their reports concerning the disorder made at the balloting and the disobedience shown on the part of the peasants with regard to the appointment of an additional balloting for the election of candidates for the office of Volost Head in the Samoylovka Society, suppose the cause of this to be the seeking of the office of Volost Head by the peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka Semyon Vyskubov with the aid of his own brother Ilya Vyskubov and some other of their fellow-members of the society, disposed in the Vyskubovs' favor and assisting them in directing the other villagers to the fulfillment of their wish. Such a conclusion is based on this: first, that the peasants Semyon and Ilya Vyskubov during the balloting allowed themselves to appear at the village assembly without any need and having for this neither the permission of the local authorities nor the right by law, being under trial, and moreover knowing of this from the government's regulations, since the first of them, Semyon, is literate, and the latter, Ilya, himself served in the office of Head; second, Semyon Vyskubov, knowing of the non-conclusion of the case for which he is under trial, allowed himself to announce at the assembly his release from trial and to give to the note issued to him by the Chancellery of the Saratov Criminal Court concerning a single appearance of his at the Chamber to answer in the case the sense of a decree of the authorities vindicating him in the said case, whereas he, as a literate man, could not but understand the content and significance of the said note, through which the wish was aroused among the electors to ballot Vyskubov for volost Head; third, Semyon Vyskubov confirmed his seeking of the office of Volost Head by his appearance at the volost balloting without any need and at a time when he, upon the admonition of the Chamber's Assessor, had finally renounced the present office; fourth, that Semyon and Ilya Vyskubov formed a party in their favor is proved by the fact that many electors remained unconvinced of their correct action despite the repeated and clear admonitions of the authorities, which they could not have allowed themselves without some outside influence upon them, and would probably have followed the example of those electors who at the very first admonition expressed willingness to fulfill the lawful demand of the authorities. Moreover, an occasion for the conviction of the Vyskubovs' forming of a party is provided by the like action of Ilya Vyskubov at the balloting of 1849; and finally, fifth, the persistent wish of certain electors, named in the reports, to keep Semyon Vyskubov as a candidate for the office of Volost Head proves that they, before others, acted in the Vyskubovs' favor and were their accomplices.

By the investigation conducted it has been discovered:

1st, the proposal to ballot Semyon Vyskubov as a candidate for the office of Volost Head was made by some electors after the announcement at the assembly by Vyskubov's cousin Ivan Vyskubov, also among the electors, who had been in the city of Saratov together with the first, that Semyon Vyskubov's case was concluded in his favor and he was released from trial, of which he had a paper. This announcement was confirmed by Semyon Vyskubov too, because upon his appearance at the assembly he did not explain to the electors, in answer to their question, the state of his case and the significance of the note given to him from the Chancellery of the Criminal Chamber, whereas he himself knew this positively, through which Vyskubov left the electors in their trust of the words about his release from trial, with the conviction of the possibility of balloting him, Vyskubov, for the office of Volost Head, in which conviction the arbitrary balloting was also made. Vyskubov's present action and his suspicious presence over the course of two days during the balloting on the square near the assembly under the pretext of buying medicine, and then his appearance without any need at the volost balloting contrary to the promise given upon the admonition of the Chamber's Assessor, and finally his action at the Volost assembly too, consisting, first, in demanding of the Volost Head Bozhkov an explanation of the cause of his not being balloted for Volost Head, whereas he knew the order given about this by the Chamber's Assessor; second, in petitioning for the giving to him of a new resolution as to conduct, of which he had no need whatever; and third, in his explanation with the electors concerning the balloting of the volost Heads — leave Vyskubov under strong suspicion of seeking the office of Volost Head, an inclination to which he disclosed by the above-described actions, the consequence of which, as must be supposed, was the arbitrary balloting and the disobedience shown on the part of many electors against the admonition of the authorities.

2nd, the brothers of Semyon Vyskubov, his cousin Ivan and his own brother Ilya, disclosed themselves to be persons acting in favor of the first, because the first of them made at the assembly a false announcement about the state of Semyon Vyskubov's case, and probably not without intention, for he, having been in the city of Saratov together with his brother, could not but know positively the course of the case, since it concerned his relative, out of attachment to whom he had made the very journey to Saratov; and the latter (Ilya Vyskubov) twice appeared during the balloting at the village assembly and conversed with the electors. Although he, Ilya Vyskubov, gives as the cause of his appearance at the assembly his summons there by the electors, this assertion is confirmed only by three electors — Danila Sayenkov, Anofry Sivolapov, and Nikifor Dubovenkov, of whom the two first at the outset themselves refuted Vyskubov's testimony. The confused statements of these electors about the inviting of Vyskubov to the assembly in consequence of the supposed common wish of all the electors, which, however, none of the questioned peasants, more than 200 in number, affirmed, give occasion to suspect Ilya Vyskubov of an arbitrary appearance at the assembly with the aim of acting in favor of his brother Semyon, because they both appeared at the assembly at the same time. This suspicion becomes still more probable when attention is turned to the actions of Ilya Vyskubov at the balloting of 1849, in which he was suspected of supposedly having made a promise to the peasants not to collect the taxes in a timely manner and to issue them grain from the granaries immediately upon his entry into the office of Volost Head, for which he was then balloting. By the investigation, the former District Chief Mr. Shonf supposes the disorder made at the balloting at that time, in which the peasants, as in the present case, showed disobedience in the conduct of new elections and disclosed a wish to keep Vyskubov as Volost Head. Consequently, the described actions of Ilya Vyskubov give occasion to suspect that the forming of parties at elections and the seeking of offices is no new thing for him, and that with such a disposition he could now have drawn his younger brother Semyon into a like action too, who was noticed in disobedience to the Authorities upon his appointment to the office of tax collector, for which he was handed over to the criminal court.

3rd, the existence of a party in favor of Vyskubov, though not positively explained by the investigation, but the indefinite and entirely concordant testimonies of almost all those electors who showed disobedience in the conduct of the additional balloting, as also their common reference, with regard to their disobedience, to the will of the Society, which they themselves constitute, prove that the evasion of these electors from the balloting of new candidates followed not from the conviction, as they declared, that the balloting already made by them is lawful, but from some other prompting, which as a common action is resolved by suspicion in one direction — to have Semyon Vyskubov as Volost Head. The explained direction was obstinately and openly disclosed by the below-named electors, whose actions consisted in the following:

Yefrem Korsunov, Yefrem Bondarenkov, Danila Kravtsov, Fyodor Bebeshkov, Vasily Khoroshevsky, Danila Sayenkov, Anofry Sivolapov, Mikhayla Kravtsov, Yakov Sharkov, Dmitry Ryasnyansky, Zakhar Natalichev, and Spiridon Shakhmatov. The first demanded the balloting of Semyon Vyskubov for Volost Head and, before others, showed disobedience and thereby maintained it in the rest against the additional balloting. Moreover, the said peasants Sharkov, Ryasnyansky, Natalichev, and Mikhayla Kravtsov even during the conduct of the investigation allowed themselves to make a disturbance at the assembly, starting a quarrel and reproaching the electors of the smaller party, who agreed to the additional balloting, with having acted badly in not joining the large party, and through this division having brought them into liability. The actions of Sharkov, Ryasnyansky, Natalichev, and Kravtsov, suppressed by their being taken under arrest, were made with fury in the sight of many electors and are confirmed by the testimonies of the sworn witnesses. Then those who showed themselves more active are: a) Yefrem Bondarenkov and Yefrem Korsunov, who in the presence of the Chamber's Assessor publicly and first refused at the assembly to carry out his proposal to conduct an additional balloting, and then, after the departure of the Chamber's Assessor from the settlement of Samoylovka, appeared at the volost balloting and, with the participation of the electors Anofry Sivolapov, Fyodor Bebeshkov, Danila Kravtsov, and the rank-and-file peasant Yakov Sorokin, again made a disorder at the volost assembly too, demanding of Head Bozhkov the balloting of Semyon Vyskubov; and when they were refused in this, they allowed themselves administrative acts in the Volost Administration by demanding ink and paper to make a list of those electors who expressed agreement to the balloting of Vyskubov; b) Vasily Khoroshevsky, who together with the elector Danila Sayenkov took part in dealings with the Vyskubovs; and c) Nikita Shcherbakov and Fyodor Shulekin — the first [took part] in all the disorders at the volost balloting, and the latter at the conduct of the investigation by his testimony that he was not in agreement before to the additional balloting and now does not wish to conduct it.

Considering the above and determining the degree of guilt and action of all the said persons, they Resolved: The elector Peasants Yakov Sharkov, Dmitry Ryasnyansky, Zakhar Natalichev, Mikhayla Kravtsov, Yefrem Bondarenkov, and Yefrem Korsunov, as the chief instigators of the disorders, who showed manifest disobedience and insubordination to the Authorities and disturbed the quiet and peace at the assembly during the conduct of the investigation, to confine, in accordance with the prescription of His Excellency the Chief of the Province of last February, No. 573, under guard in the prison castle. Then the electors less convicted by the investigation begun of disorders in the present case — Anofry Sivolapov, Ivan Vyskubov, Danila Sayenkov, Vasily Khoroshevsky, Fyodor Bebeshkov, Danila Kravtsov, and the rank-and-file peasants Semyon and Ilya Vyskubov and Yakov Sorokin — to take from the Society and place under the supervision of the Rural Police until the conclusion of the case and the discretion of the Authorities, with the aim of suppressing any action and influence on their part during the further conduct of the investigation. And the Vyskubovs — Spiridon Shakhmatov, Nikita Shcherbakov, and Fyodor Shulekin — to place under the supervision of the local Police constable, in consideration that the first of them withdrew from the Vyskubovs' party and expressed willingness for a new balloting, and the latter, as people of limited understanding, showed their obstinacy rather by the example of others than from their own conviction. Of which to record in the journal and to carry it into execution. Official for Special Commissions Sivtsov. District Chief Volotsky.

> Received 14 March 1858.

In reply to the questions proposed by Your High Nobility I have the honor to explain that during the conduct of the village balloting in the settlement of Samoylovka I did not through anyone invite the peasant Semyon Vyskubov to the assembly and demanded no note from him, but he of his own accord appeared at the assembly, where he himself presented to the electors the receipt given to him from the chancellery of the Criminal Chamber, which I, by order of the Village Elder, read at the assembly and returned to Vyskubov. With regard to the unlawful acts made at the balloting, the following is known: during the consultation of the electors on the designation of candidates for the office of Volost Head, a talk arose at the assembly that Semyon [Vyskubov] should be balloted for head, or his brother Ilya Vyskubov; this wish was expressed by very many electors, but on what occasion and who first raised such a proposal — I did not notice. In consequence of this wish, Elder Troshchilov verbally announced to the electors that the Vyskubovs, being under trial, cannot be admitted to the balloting; at this time there appeared at the assembly, at the balloting table, as I suppose arbitrarily, Semyon Vyskubov and, addressing the electors, said: gentlemen, the old men say that I am under trial, but that is not true, I am acquitted, of which I have a decree. On this account Elder Troshchilov charged me to read at the assembly the paper presented by Vyskubov, which proved to be a note of the Chancellery of the Criminal Chamber about a single appearance of Vyskubov there, and then Troshchilov again announced to the assembly the impossibility of balloting Semyon Vyskubov, and in attestation of this there was read by me at the assembly the decree of the Chamber of State Domains on the handing over of Vyskubov to Trial; but the greater part of the electors, trusting for some reason the words of Vyskubov, who at that time had gone away, announced their wish to ballot him without fail, which they also carried out arbitrarily. In this action there acted substantially Yefrem Korsunov, Yefrem Bondarenkov, Spiridon Shakhmatov, and some others whom I did not notice, because on account of the disorder that arose I went out of the assembly into the Village Administration. During the balloting Semyon Vyskubov's brother Ilya also appeared at the assembly without any need, and was now near the assembly, now in the entry of the Administration. On what occasion the electors wished to ballot Vyskubov for head — is unknown to me, and whether there were at this any secret actions and consultations — I do not know. The words said by Semyon Vyskubov at the assembly concerning his being under trial were, I suppose, heard and can be confirmed by the village Headmen Yefrem Dolzhenkov and Taras Beskrovny, and the Trustworthy man of the Village Court. March the 17th day 1858. The former Samoylovka Clerk, now assistant of the Turki Volost clerk, Sokolov. Official for Special Commissions Sivtsov.

Question points. On the [illegible] day of March 1857, by the Balashov Land Captain, in the presence of a deputy on the part of the State peasants, given to the below-written man, to which he must give answers:

Questions. — Answers.

On the 1st: What is your name, patronymic, and surname, how many [years] are you, do you know how to read and write, what faith are you, if Orthodox, then do you attend confession and Holy Communion, were you for anything under trial and punished, and if you were, then how was the case concluded, and where are you recorded according to the registers of the [9th] census. — Answer: My name is Semyon, [son] of Trofim, surnamed Vyskubov, I am 35 years of age, of the Orthodox faith, I go to God's Church, attend confession and Holy Communion, do not know letters, was not under Trial or punished, a State peasant of the settlement of Samoylovka, where I am also recorded according to the registers of the 9th census.

On the 2nd: In 1856 were you chosen by the society of the settlement of Samoylovka as tax collector and confirmed in that [office]; if you were chosen and confirmed in the office, then why, not entering upon the carrying out of it, did you willfully go off to the city of Saratov, and upon your return, [although] you did take the oath, completely refused to carry out the office [of collector]; was the prescription of the District Chief announced to you [through] the Volost Administration, by which it was prescribed to impress upon the Samoylovka society that [he], while carrying out the duty of collector, may also be proxy; was it precisely that, at the gathering of 187 of your fellow-society-members at the assembly, 49 men did not express a wish to admit you to the carrying out of the office of tax collector, and 138 men declared that they have no obstacle whatever to your entering as collector and who [it seems] consider your evasion unjust, the more so as you were not elected by them as proxy and have no power of attorney on this matter; and was the prescription of the District Chief, which followed upon the prescription of the Chamber of State Domains of 14 June, No. 12276, announced to you, that if you [refuse] to accept the office of collector, you will be handed over to trial for evasion and disobedience; and was it precisely that you refused to accept the office of tax collector and did not agree to give a pledge of hearing that. — Answer: On 24 February 1856 [it was announced] that I was confirmed in the said office and obliged to enter upon the carrying out of it; then, when it was announced to me in the village court at a full village assembly, as [I now] recall, at the assembly my fellow-society-members said that they had elected me proxy in the case with head Nechayev regarding his improper handing-over of communal land plots, and did not admit me to the office of tax collector, but would elect another; therefore I refused to accept the duty of collector imposed on me. And indeed, in December 1855 or in January 1856 I went to Saratov on my own needs, and soon, namely on the 29th of that same February, I fell ill and was in severe illness until the feast of Holy Pascha, which is known to our parish priest Fyodor Vinogradov; during [the illness], it seems, in the month of March, the Assistant of the Balashov district chief came to the settlement of Samoylovka and summoned me, to announce to me that I, as confirmed by the [Chamber] of State Domains in the office of tax collector, [must take] the oath and enter upon the carrying out of the duty, wherefore [I] was at the same time brought to the oath in our church, but upon the carrying out of the office, on account of intensified illness, I could not enter. And it was precisely that after Holy Pascha, on the second or third day, I received relief from the illness and [was] summoned to the village administration, where the elder announced to me that, [having] received relief from the illness, I should without fail enter upon the carrying out of the duty of tax collector; but I demanded that a full assembly be gathered, and when the majority of the society should adjudge me to enter upon the carrying out of the office, then I would agree; but my demand was not fulfilled. Soon after this, as far as I recall, in [the month of] May, I set off to the city of Saratov on my own need, and, on returning from there, heard from my own brother Ilya Trofimov that in my absence Mr. District Chief of State Domains had been in our settlement and had summoned my brother, whom he ordered to announce to me that I might carry out both the duties of proxy and the office of [collector]; but whether my brother told Mr. District Chief that the society forbids me to enter the office, that I do not know and did not hear from my brother. As I indeed did not enter the office, because I considered this against the wish of the society, then by disposition of Mr. District Chief a village assembly was gathered, and at this time I was at home. At this assembly there were not 187 men, as reported, but only 76 men, of which number 48 men affirmed that they indeed do not wish to admit me to the carrying out of the duty of tax collector, for which they will elect another, and entrust to me petitioning in the case of the unlawful acts of head Nechayev; and 28 men testified the contrary. A power-of-attorney resolution, attested in the proper form, I did not have, but there was given to me a private power of attorney from my fellow-society-members, which was taken by the Chamber Official Mr. Popov, and there remained with me only the draft power of attorney, which I also present; the prescription of the Chamber of State Domains of 14 June, No. 12276, by which it was prescribed to announce to me that if I do not accept the office of collector, I will be handed over to trial for evasion and disobedience, was announced to me by no one.

On the 3rd: To all the questions given me I answer the very justice, without concealment or addition. The State peasant Semyon Trofimov Vyskubov set his hand. [illegible]. Assistant District Chief [illegible].
